## From professional experience to software

My projects include property-management software, homelessness prevention, housing assessments and supported-housing research. They are at different stages of development; a working prototype is not a claim of production readiness.

My prototypes have attracted interest from senior decision-makers outside my organisation. That interest is encouraging, but does not mean the applications are endorsed or proven effective. My first offer of paid development work led to the establishment of EnantiomorphAI as a sole-trader business in October 2026.

## What this journal explores

- How professional expertise can become useful software through AI-assisted development.
- Working with ChatGPT to explore ideas and requirements, and with Codex on implementation, code examination and testing.
- Lessons from design, debugging, testing and responsible AI use.
- The work required to turn prototypes into secure, dependable production systems, including recognising when independent engineering review is needed.
- Honest successes, mistakes, limitations and unanswered questions.

I use “AI-native developer” to describe an approach in which AI is part of development from the outset. It is not a claim of engineering seniority.

## Who this is for

This journal is for curious professionals from any industry, particularly those with substantial domain expertise but no traditional software-engineering background. It is also an invitation to experienced developers to share perspectives and help deepen the discussion.

I'm learning in public, not presenting a finished portfolio or claiming to have all the answers.
